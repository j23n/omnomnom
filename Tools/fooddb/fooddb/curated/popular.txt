# Everyday foods, one database name per line, most common first.
#
# Written against the real Bundeslebensmittelschlüssel 4.0 names. It was previously
# written against FDC descriptions, which matched nothing once the bundle moved to
# European sources — 47 of 48 entries found no food at all, so the ranking prior was
# silently zero everywhere and auto-matching had nothing but text length to break a tie.
#
# Matching is case-insensitive, whitespace-collapsed, and ignores anything in brackets,
# so "Coffee (infusion)" is reached by writing either form. Entries that match no food
# are logged as warnings, not errors, so a list can outlive a source change.
#
# What belongs here: the form of a food a person means when they type its bare name.
# "Coffee" means the drink, not the ice cream; "milk" means milk, not milk chocolate.
# That is the whole job of this file, and it is why the prior has to be strong enough to
# beat a better *text* match: "Milk chocolate" really does start with "milk".
